# Cybersecurity-Risk-Register-GRC-Portfolio-Project
Cybersecurity Risk Register | GRC Portfolio Project

**Project Overview**

This project demonstrates my foundational understanding of Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) through a cybersecurity risk register developed for BrightPath Digital Services Ltd., a fictional digital services company.

The objective is to demonstrate how cybersecurity risks can be identified, assessed, documented, and assigned proposed treatment actions.

The project uses illustrative scenarios to practise risk documentation and qualitative risk assessment.

**Business Scenario**

BrightPath Digital Services Ltd. is a hypothetical digital services company that relies on employee accounts, customer information, cloud storage, administrative accounts, and business applications.

For this exercise, I considered cybersecurity risks that could affect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of its information and systems.

The scenarios are training examples, not verified findings from a real organisation.

**Project Objectives**

* Identify potential cybersecurity risks affecting business operations.
* Write structured risk statements using cause, event, and consequence.
* Assess likelihood and impact using qualitative ratings.
* Apply an illustrative risk matrix.
* Recommend practical risk treatment actions.
* Identify proposed risk owners and action owners.
* Document the importance of evidence and remediation tracking.

**Risk Scenarios**

The workbook covers eight illustrative scenarios:

1. Weak employee passwords.
2. Former employee accounts remaining active.
3. Excessive user permissions.
4. Shared administrator accounts.
5. Backup and recovery readiness not verified.
6. Incomplete multi-factor authentication (MFA) coverage.
7. Insufficient monitoring of suspicious sign-ins.
8. Cloud storage sharing permissions that may be too broad.

**Methodology**

The risk register uses a qualitative approach based on likelihood and impact.

Each risk includes a structured statement describing its cause, potential event, and consequences. Proposed treatments identify actions that could reduce the likelihood or impact of the risk.

The workbook also includes proposed ownership, status tracking, and fields for treatment evidence.

The initial ratings are illustrative and require validation. In a real engagement, the assessment would use available evidence, agreed rating criteria, and the organisation’s approved risk assessment methodology.

## Risk Assessment Methodology

This project uses a qualitative three-level risk assessment methodology to identify, evaluate and prioritise cybersecurity risks.

### 1. Likelihood Scale

| Rating | Definition |
|---|---|
| Low | The risk event is unlikely to occur under normal circumstances. |
| Moderate | The risk event could occur under certain circumstances. |
| High | The risk event is likely to occur or may occur repeatedly. |

### 2. Impact Scale

| Rating | Definition |
|---|---|
| Low | Minimal business disruption, financial impact or information exposure. |
| Moderate | Noticeable business disruption, financial loss or exposure of internal information. |
| High | Serious business disruption, significant financial loss, sensitive data exposure or legal consequences. |

### 3. Risk Scoring Matrix

The overall risk rating is determined by combining the likelihood and impact ratings.

| Likelihood / Impact | Low | Moderate | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Low | Low | Moderate |
| Moderate | Low | Moderate | High |
| High | Moderate | High | High |

### 4. Risk Classification

| Risk Rating | Priority | General Response |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Routine | Maintain existing controls and monitor. |
| Moderate | Medium | Identify appropriate treatment actions and monitor progress. |
| High | High | Prioritise treatment and escalate to the appropriate risk owner. |

### 5. Inherent Risk

Inherent risk represents the level of risk before considering the effectiveness of existing security controls.

### 6. Residual Risk

Residual risk represents the level of risk remaining after treatment measures have been implemented and their effectiveness assessed.

Residual risk must not be reduced simply because a control has been recommended. Evidence of implementation and effectiveness is required before reassessment.

### 7. Risk Assessment Principles

- Risk ratings are based on documented assumptions and available evidence.
- Unknown control effectiveness must not be treated as confirmed protection.
- Recommended treatments do not automatically reduce risk ratings.
- Likelihood and impact must be reassessed after treatment.
- Risk owners are responsible for reviewing and accepting residual risks.
- Risk assessments should be reviewed periodically and when significant changes occur.

### Disclaimer

This methodology is used for fictional learning and portfolio projects. The ratings are illustrative and do not represent verified organisational risk assessments. Actual organisations should use their approved risk assessment criteria.

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 Mapping

The cybersecurity risks documented in this project are mapped to relevant NIST CSF 2.0 subcategories.

| Identified Risk | NIST CSF 2.0 Subcategory | Relevant Security Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Weak employee passwords | PR.AA-03 | Users, services and hardware are authenticated. |
| Former employee accounts remain active | PR.AA-01, PR.AA-05 | Identities and credentials are managed; access permissions are reviewed and revoked when no longer required. |
| Excessive user permissions | PR.AA-05 | Access permissions are managed and reviewed using least privilege. |
| Shared administrator accounts | PR.AA-01, PR.AA-05 | Identities and credentials are managed; access permissions follow least privilege. |
| Backup recovery readiness not verified | PR.DS-11 | Backups of data are created, protected, maintained and tested. |
| Incomplete MFA coverage | PR.AA-03 | Users, services and hardware are authenticated. |
| Insufficient monitoring of suspicious sign-ins | DE.CM-03 | Personnel activity and technology usage are monitored to find potentially adverse events. |
| Cloud storage sharing permissions too broad | PR.AA-05, PR.DS-01 | Access permissions are managed; data at rest is protected. |

### Mapping Purpose

The mapping connects identified cybersecurity risks to relevant NIST CSF 2.0 security outcomes. It supports the selection of appropriate risk treatment actions and helps organise security improvement activities.

**Note:** A mapped subcategory does not mean a control has been implemented. Actual implementation and effectiveness require supporting evidence.
