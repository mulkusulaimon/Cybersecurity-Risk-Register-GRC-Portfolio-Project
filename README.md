# Cybersecurity-Risk-Register-GRC-Portfolio-Project
Cybersecurity Risk Register | GRC Portfolio Project

**Project Overview**

This project demonstrates my foundational understanding of Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) through a cybersecurity risk register developed for BrightPath Digital Services Ltd., a fictional digital services company.

The objective is to demonstrate how cybersecurity risks can be identified, assessed, documented, and assigned proposed treatment actions.

The project uses illustrative scenarios to practise risk documentation and qualitative risk assessment.

**Business Scenario**

BrightPath Digital Services Ltd. is a hypothetical digital services company that relies on employee accounts, customer information, cloud storage, administrative accounts, and business applications.

For this exercise, I considered cybersecurity risks that could affect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of its information and systems.

The scenarios are training examples, not verified findings from a real organisation.

**Project Objectives**

* Identify potential cybersecurity risks affecting business operations.
* Write structured risk statements using cause, event, and consequence.
* Assess likelihood and impact using qualitative ratings.
* Apply an illustrative risk matrix.
* Recommend practical risk treatment actions.
* Identify proposed risk owners and action owners.
* Document the importance of evidence and remediation tracking.

**Risk Scenarios**

The workbook covers eight illustrative scenarios:

1. Weak employee passwords.
2. Former employee accounts remaining active.
3. Excessive user permissions.
4. Shared administrator accounts.
5. Backup and recovery readiness not verified.
6. Incomplete multi-factor authentication (MFA) coverage.
7. Insufficient monitoring of suspicious sign-ins.
8. Cloud storage sharing permissions that may be too broad.

**Methodology**

The risk register uses a qualitative approach based on likelihood and impact.

Each risk includes a structured statement describing its cause, potential event, and consequences. Proposed treatments identify actions that could reduce the likelihood or impact of the risk.

The workbook also includes proposed ownership, status tracking, and fields for treatment evidence.

The initial ratings are illustrative and require validation. In a real engagement, the assessment would use available evidence, agreed rating criteria, and the organisation’s approved risk assessment methodology.

**Limitations**

This is a beginner portfolio exercise using a fictional company. No real security assessment, audit, control testing, or client engagement was conducted.

The risks, ratings, owners, and proposed actions are illustrative. They should not be interpreted as confirmed security weaknesses.

**Learning Outcome**

Through this exercise, I practised documenting cybersecurity risks, distinguishing assumptions from verified evidence, and linking identified risks to practical treatment actions.

My next learning objective is to improve the consistency of risk ratings and strengthen the supporting methodology.
